# Victor Robert Book Marketing Agency
## Original website strategy and reference synthesis

**Document status:** Recommended planning document based on supplied research results. This is not a reproduction of the reference site and does not assert unverified Victor Robert credentials, clients, outcomes, prices, or vendor decisions.

---

## Executive recommendation

Build Victor Robert as a **premium editorial specialist**: a calm, evidence-minded partner for authors and publishers who need a clear path from book positioning to discoverability, launch, and sustained reader demand. The site should feel closer to a well-edited literary imprint or strategy journal than a generic performance-marketing agency.

The recommended experience is a deliberately paced conversion narrative:

1. **Recognize the author’s problem** — a strong book can still be difficult to discover.
2. **See the point of view** — Victor Robert brings editorial judgment, market intelligence, and disciplined execution.
3. **Understand the offer** — strategy, launch, discoverability, author platform, paid amplification, and ongoing growth are explained in plain language.
4. **Verify fit** — real case studies, attributed testimonials, methodology, boundaries, and transparent investment guidance are shown only when documented.
5. **Choose a comfortable next step** — send a project enquiry, request a strategy conversation, or use a confirmed scheduling link.

Use one primary action consistently: **Start a project conversation**. Support it with two lower-friction alternatives: **See how the work works** and **Ask a question**. Do not use urgency, guaranteed sales language, fabricated logos, placeholder testimonials presented as proof, or unverified performance statistics.

> **Working positioning placeholder:** “Book marketing with editorial judgment.”
>
> This line is a direction to validate with Victor Robert, not approved final copy.

### 1.2 Limitations and confidence boundaries

The supplied research was based on **static fetches, extracted HTML/text, and limited source inspection**. It did not constitute a full website audit. The following remain unverified:

- Exact visual styling, image crops, responsive layout, hover and focus states, animation timing, and behavior across real devices.
- Screen-reader output, keyboard-only flow, focus trapping, Escape behavior, touch-target sizing, contrast, zoom behavior, and full WCAG conformance.
- Form submission, validation, confirmation/error states, spam prevention, provider notifications, redirect behavior, analytics consent blocking, and data retention.
- External calendar availability/completion, WhatsApp behavior, and any claimed response times.
- Authenticity, authorization, methodology, or performance of reference testimonials, metrics, client logos, campaign results, prices, and other business claims.
- Whether all observed source behavior is still live or represents a production-standard implementation.

**Guardrail:** the reference is useful as an information-architecture and conversion-pattern study only. Victor Robert must use original copy, original visual design, original proof, actual business terms, and confirmed integrations.

## 3. Page-by-page section and function inventory

### 3.1 Homepage `/`

1. **Announcement or quiet utility bar (optional):** only for a real announcement; otherwise omit to preserve premium restraint.
2. **Sticky header:** preserved Victor Robert logo, compact navigation, primary “Start a project conversation” button; visible scrolled state.
3. **Editorial hero:** one strong positioning statement, short qualifier, primary CTA, secondary “Explore the approach” link; no unsupported superlatives.
4. **Selected signal strip:** use only confirmed facts, such as service focus, years, books supported, or geography. Until verified, replace with a point-of-view sentence rather than fake metrics.
5. **Problem framing:** explain why good books need more than isolated promotion; distinguish discoverability, positioning, launch, and long-tail growth.
6. **Service constellation:** six concise service families linking to detail routes. Avoid a noisy keyword marquee unless it has a clear pause/reduced-motion implementation.
7. **Method preview:** four to six stages linking to `/process/`.
8. **Featured work:** two or three approved case-study cards with “context / intervention / evidence” labels. If no approved work exists, use an editorial “How we assess an opportunity” module instead.
9. **Founder note:** short Victor Robert introduction linking to `/about/`.
10. **Resource capture:** one real, useful guide with minimal fields; show what it contains and actual delivery method. Omit until ready.
11. **FAQ preview:** four to six high-risk questions with accessible disclosure controls; link to `/faq/`.
12. **Final CTA:** choice of project enquiry, conversation, and question route.
13. **Footer:** logo, compact navigation, service links, legal, confirmed contact channels, social links only where active, and copyright.
14. **Persistent contact control (optional):** only if a real channel is monitored; label it, make it keyboard accessible, and avoid overlap with mobile browser UI.

### 3.2 About `/about/`

- Hero with actual role and a restrained point of view.
- “Why this work” origin story, written in Victor Robert’s own voice.
- Editorial principles: clarity, audience respect, evidence, sustainable momentum, and honest boundaries.
- Founder capability matrix: only confirmed experience and credentials; link to evidence where possible.
- Working model: what Victor Robert personally handles, what may be delegated, and how collaboration works.
- “Who this is for / not for” qualification block.
- Portrait or studio image only if supplied and approved; provide accurate alt text.
- CTA to process, services, and conversation.

### 3.3 Services hub `/services/`

- Intro: services are connected parts of a book’s market journey, not a menu of disconnected tactics.
- Six service-family cards: book positioning; launch strategy; Amazon/discoverability; author platform; paid amplification; ongoing growth.
- Each card: problem solved, typical outputs, best fit, dependencies, and route.
- Engagement models: diagnostic, focused project, launch partnership, ongoing advisory — **names and availability are placeholders until confirmed**.
- “What we do not promise” section: no guaranteed rankings, sales, reviews, bestseller status, or fixed outcomes unless legally and commercially approved.
- Process preview and CTA.

### 3.4 Service detail template `/services/[slug]/`

Reusable content fields:

- Service title and one-sentence outcome-oriented description.
- Best-fit author/book situation.
- What the service changes and what it does not control.
- Deliverables grouped by phase.
- Inputs required from the author/publisher.
- Timing shown as a planning range only after Victor Robert confirms capacity.
- Engagement format and scope boundary.
- Measurement plan: leading indicators, lagging indicators, reporting cadence.
- Related services.
- FAQ block specific to the service.
- CTA to `/contact/` and `/conversation/`.

### 3.5 Work index `/work/`

- Intro explaining that published work is selected for learning value, not a promise of identical outcomes.
- Featured case cards with book/genre, challenge, intervention, and one verified evidence point.
- Optional taxonomy by objective (positioning, launch, discoverability, platform, paid) only when there are enough cases to make filters useful.
- Empty-state alternative: a transparent “case studies are being documented” note plus methodology link, never invented proof.
- CTA to discuss a comparable situation.

### 3.6 Case-study detail `/work/[slug]/`

- Metadata: author/publisher permission status, book category, period, scope, and role.
- Context and baseline.
- Challenge and constraints.
- Strategic diagnosis.
- Work delivered, with artifacts where permission allows.
- Execution timeline and decision points.
- Results: exact metric definitions, source/method, measurement window, and caveat.
- What was not controlled.
- Client quote only with written permission and attribution choice.
- Lessons for similar books.
- CTA, plus related work.

### 3.7 Testimonials `/testimonials/`

- Intro and consent/attribution standard.
- Small set of real quotes, each with name, role, book/project context, date or engagement context where appropriate, and permission status.
- Link from a quote to a fuller case study only if the case exists.
- Private feedback route for clients, not presented as public proof.
- If no approved testimonials exist, replace the page with a “How we work” evidence page or keep it out of navigation.

### 3.8 Process `/process/`

Recommended stages:

1. **Signal:** enquiry and initial fit review.
2. **Read:** book, audience, category, current assets, and constraints.
3. **Diagnose:** identify the highest-leverage opportunity.
4. **Recommend:** outline priorities, sequencing, and measurement.
5. **Scope:** confirm deliverables, responsibilities, timing, and investment.
6. **Build:** create or refine assets and campaign structure.
7. **Activate:** launch with agreed channels and approvals.
8. **Learn:** review signals, improve what is controllable, report clearly.
9. **Decide:** close, extend, or shift the work based on evidence.

Each stage should show owner, output, client input, and decision gate. These stage names are original recommendations and must be adapted to Victor Robert’s actual workflow.

### 3.9 Investment `/investment/`

- Explain how work is scoped: diagnostic, fixed-scope project, launch partnership, or ongoing advisory — confirm actual model first.
- Separate Victor Robert fees from third-party costs and paid media.
- State what determines investment: book stage, complexity, channels, asset condition, approval load, and duration.
- If publishing ranges, label them as current planning ranges with effective date and scope caveat.
- Explain proposal process, payment schedule, cancellation, and what is excluded only after business confirmation.
- Include “not sure of budget?” route to a conversation.
- No checkout is recommended for a specialist service at launch.

### 3.10 FAQ `/faq/`

Group by decision risk:

- **Fit:** Is this for traditionally published, hybrid, or independent authors? Genres? Debut or backlist?
- **Readiness:** What must exist before work begins?
- **Scope:** What does Victor Robert personally handle?
- **Timing:** How are dates planned around publication and launch windows?
- **Channels:** Which channels are considered and how are they selected?
- **Measurement:** What can be measured, and what cannot be promised?
- **Investment:** How are fees, media, and third-party costs treated?
- **Collaboration:** What approvals and author time are needed?
- **Privacy:** How are enquiry and mailing-list details used?

Use native buttons or properly implemented disclosure components, not clickable generic `div`s. Include a static link alternative to the contact route.

### 3.11 Contact `/contact/`

- Contextual heading: “Tell us what the book needs next.”
- Detailed fields, subject to final CRM/privacy decision: name, email, role, book title, genre/category, format, publication stage/date, current assets/URL, goals, services of interest, budget comfort (including “not sure”), and message.
- Explicitly state who receives the submission and what the next step is; use a placeholder until confirmed.
- Consent checkbox only where legally/business required, with separate optional marketing consent.
- Privacy link and no-guarantee/outcome note.
- Alternative email and monitored messaging route if real.
- Accessible errors, confirmation, spam protection, and server-side validation.

### 3.12 Conversation `/conversation/`

- Shorter form than Contact: name, email, book/project, one priority, preferred timing, and optional context.
- What the conversation covers and what it does not: diagnosis and fit, not a guaranteed sales forecast.
- Scheduling embed or external link only after provider and calendar ownership are confirmed.
- Non-calendar fallback to submit the form.
- Confirmation route with a clear next step.

### 3.13 Insights and resources

Launch only with real content. An article template should include title, standfirst, author, date, category, reading time only if calculated, body, cited sources where applicable, related service, and one contextual CTA. A resource template should explain the asset, who it is for, file/privacy handling, and delivery. Never collect an email for an asset that has not been produced and tested.

### 3.14 Legal and utility pages

- Privacy: actual cookies, analytics, form, scheduler, CRM, email, messaging, hosting, retention, rights, and international transfers.
- Terms: actual website and service terms, jurisdiction, intellectual-property boundaries, disclaimers.
- Accessibility: commitment, known limitations, contact path, and review date.
- Thank-you: confirmation and useful next routes without invented SLAs.

## 5. Conversion journey

### Journey A: ready-to-start author

`Homepage → relevant service → process/investment reassurance → project enquiry → confirmation → human review → scoped recommendation`

- Primary CTA: **Start a project conversation**.
- Form should qualify without demanding a full proposal brief.
- Confirmation should state the actual next step once Victor Robert confirms the operating model.

### Journey B: proof-led evaluator

`Homepage → work index → case study → related service → conversation/contact`

- Lead with evidence and context, not a hard sell.
- Every case study should explain what was controlled and what was not.
- If no approved cases are available, route to method and insight content instead of fake proof.

### Journey C: early-stage or uncertain author

`Homepage → about/process → FAQ → question route or resource → later conversation`

- Use low-friction “Ask a question” action.
- Resource capture is optional and must be attached to a real asset and compliant consent flow.
- Do not pressure an early-stage visitor into a long form.

### Journey D: returning visitor / referral

`Direct service/work URL → concise context → CTA → form or scheduler`

- Every interior page needs a clear next action.
- Use consistent metadata, breadcrumbs, related links, and mobile-safe sticky CTA if tested.

### Conversion principles

- One dominant CTA per viewport hierarchy; do not make every button equally loud.
- Repeat CTAs at natural decision points: hero, after proof/process, after FAQ, footer.
- Offer a choice of commitment, but keep the choices legible: enquiry, conversation, question.
- Pair claims with evidence or mark them as a point of view.
- Put privacy and data-use language at the moment of capture.
- Never promise a sales outcome, bestseller result, ranking, review count, or response time without approved evidence and terms.

## 6. Technical recommendation for a managed modern React project

### Recommended implementation

Use a managed React/Next.js-style project with:

- TypeScript and strict linting.
- Component-driven design system with tokens for color, type, spacing, radii, motion, and focus states.
- File-based routes matching the sitemap.
- A managed CMS or structured content layer for services, work, testimonials, FAQs, and insights; use preview/draft support and revision history.
- Static generation or server rendering for marketing pages, with incremental revalidation for CMS content.
- Optimized local image assets, responsive `srcset`, meaningful dimensions, modern formats, and descriptive alt text.
- Semantic HTML, one meaningful H1 per page, landmark regions, visible keyboard focus, skip link, accessible disclosures, reduced-motion support, and automated plus manual accessibility testing.
- Forms through a confirmed managed endpoint or server action connected to the chosen CRM/email workflow; validate server-side, rate-limit, add honeypot or managed anti-spam, and provide accessible success/error states.
- Scheduler integration as a labeled external handoff or approved embed; always retain a non-scheduler alternative.
- Consent-aware analytics with events for CTA clicks, form starts, form success, scheduler handoff, resource delivery, and outbound links. Do not load non-essential tracking before consent where required.
- SEO baseline: canonical tags, XML sitemap, robots.txt, Open Graph/Twitter metadata, clean redirects, `Organization`/`ProfessionalService`, `WebSite`, `BreadcrumbList`, `Article`, and `Service` structured data only where truthful.
- Performance targets: fast first load, minimal client JavaScript, no unnecessary animation, stable layout, and tested Core Web Vitals. Do not claim scores before testing.
- Security: HTTPS, dependency updates, secrets outside source control, least-privilege integrations, form data minimization, backups, and a documented retention/deletion process.
- Observability: deployment previews, error logging, uptime checks, form/scheduler failure alerts, and a content QA checklist.

### Suggested content and deployment workflow

1. Define approved claims and content owners.
2. Establish design tokens and accessible primitives.
3. Build shell and templates first.
4. Enter real services and proof through CMS/schema.
5. Integrate forms and scheduler in a staging environment.
6. Run content, link, metadata, accessibility, responsive, privacy, and conversion QA.
7. Launch with valid sitemap/robots and analytics consent behavior.
8. Review search queries, form quality, and drop-off monthly; improve content before adding complexity.

## 8. Brand and design direction

### Core direction

Create a **navy-and-gold editorial system** anchored by the supplied Victor Robert logo. Preserve the logo exactly as provided: do not redraw, simplify, recolor, add effects, alter proportions, or create a competing mark. Build the site around its authority rather than around a new logo concept.

The desired impression is:

- **Premium, not glossy.** Quiet confidence, generous space, tactile paper-like neutrals, and restrained gold.
- **Editorial, not ornamental.** Strong hierarchy, thoughtful captions, marginal notes, and typography that supports long-form reading.
- **Specialist, not broad.** Specific language about books, audiences, positioning, and evidence.
- **Human, not casual.** A real founder voice and clear boundaries.
- **Measured, not guaranteed.** Evidence and caveats are part of the design.

### Palette direction

Use the exact logo colors as the source of truth after inspecting the supplied asset. Until tokens are sampled and approved, use semantic placeholders rather than claiming exact brand values:

- `--color-navy-ink: [sample from supplied logo]`
- `--color-navy-deep: [derived accessible dark]`
- `--color-gold: [sample from supplied logo]`
- `--color-paper: warm near-white`
- `--color-stone: warm neutral for panels`
- `--color-ink-muted: accessible secondary text`
- `--color-line: low-contrast rule, tested for visibility`

Gold should be an accent for rules, eyebrow labels, icons, active states, and selected CTAs—not body text on white unless contrast is verified. Use navy or dark ink for readable text. Test every foreground/background pair.

### Typography and layout

- Pair a distinctive editorial serif display face with a highly legible sans-serif for body, forms, navigation, and data.
- Keep body copy at a comfortable reading measure; use a narrow column for essays and case-study narratives.
- Use a strong typographic scale, asymmetric but disciplined grids, and generous vertical rhythm.
- Prefer hairline rules, numbered sections, captions, and pull quotes over decorative badges.
- Use photography, book covers, documents, or campaign artifacts only with rights and accurate captions.
- Avoid generic stock images of people holding books, gold gradients, excessive rounded cards, and loud “agency” visual tropes.

### Motion and interaction

- Use subtle reveal and underline transitions only when they improve orientation.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; provide no essential information through motion.
- Implement accessible mobile navigation, focus return, Escape close, and disclosure state announcements.
- Ensure persistent contact controls do not obscure content or keyboard focus.

### Logo handling

- Preserve the supplied logo lockup and proportions.
- Provide adequate clear space and a minimum size based on legibility testing.
- Use the approved logo on paper, navy, and dark backgrounds only where its contrast is validated.
- Do not use an unapproved icon-only crop as a favicon or social avatar; request an approved asset if needed.
- Store the original source file and optimized web derivatives separately; do not overwrite the master.

## 9. Content and proof governance

Before publishing any claim, classify it:

1. **Fact:** supported by a current internal record or public source.
2. **Client evidence:** permissioned, attributed, and accompanied by context.
3. **Point of view:** clearly written as Victor Robert’s perspective.
4. **Placeholder:** visibly marked internally and removed or resolved before launch.
5. **Promise:** reviewed commercially and legally; avoid outcome guarantees.

Every case study and testimonial should have an owner, source, permission status, last-reviewed date, and removal process. A premium site gains credibility by showing what it knows and what it cannot control.

## 10. Recommended first build backlog

### Must have for launch

- Insights library with a real editorial cadence.
- More case studies and taxonomy filters.

### Definition of done

The site is ready when every route has approved content, every claim has a source or is clearly a point of view, every form has been tested end to end, all external handoffs are labeled and functional, keyboard and mobile flows work, legal/vendor disclosures match reality, and the logo remains unaltered.
